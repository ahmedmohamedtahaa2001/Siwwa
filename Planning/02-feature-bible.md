## 0. Executive Summary

Siwa is not a generic fragrance storefront. It is a trust-first commerce system designed to reduce perfume risk before the customer commits.

The feature strategy is built around six trust gaps that shape every purchase decision:

1. I cannot smell it before I buy it.
2. Is it authentic and real?
3. Will it last on my skin?
4. What if I hate it after buying full-size?
5. Does this feel worth the money?
6. Is this brand real or just luxury packaging?

### The three-tier structure

- Unique Selling Features: proprietary, brand-defining capabilities that no competitor delivers.
- Core Commerce Features: essential e-commerce features executed with Siwa's tone, rigor, and product strategy.
- Supporting Features: longer-term growth levers that compound after launch.

> The system is designed to work in this order: trust first, conversion second, retention third.

## USF-01 — THE ORACLE (SCENT QUIZ)

**The highest-leverage feature in the entire store.**

---

### What It Is

A guided 6-step consultation that replaces the in-store salesperson. The customer answers questions about their preferences, lifestyle, occasions, and budget. The Oracle produces a personalized shortlist of 3–5 fragrances — a mix of Originals and Impressions — with a Discovery Kit offer and a discount code on the result page.

The quiz result is saved to the customer's account. When they return, the Oracle remembers.

---

### Trust Gaps Closed

| **Gap**   | **Role**  | **Mechanism**                                                                                                                     |
| --------- | --------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Gap 1** | Primary   | Collapses 48 choices to a confident shortlist of 3–5. Replaces the salesperson.                                                   |
| **Gap 4** | Secondary | Result always includes the tester option as the default first step — builds confidence, removes remaining risk.                   |
| **Gap 5** | Tertiary  | A personalized recommendation is perceived as more valuable than a bestseller list — even when the underlying logic is identical. |

---

### The Six Questions

```
Step 1 — The Opening
"What draws you to a fragrance first?"
  › The opening note — the first breath
  › The lasting base — what lingers on skin
  › The full journey — all of it

Step 2 — The Occasion
"When do you reach for it?"
  › Every day — the quiet companion
  › Evening — the one you save
  › Travel — the one that goes everywhere
  › All occasions — the versatile crossing

Step 3 — The Presence
"How present do you want to be in a room?"
  › Intimate — a secret, close to skin
  › Present — noticed, not announced
  › Announced — unmistakably there

Step 4 — The Landscape
"Which landscape calls to you?"
  › Salt flats and dry mineral air
  › Date palms and warm resin
  › The garden inside the desert — fresh florals
  › Smoke and ancient wood

Step 5 — The Commitment
"How do you want to arrive?"
  › The Original — at full depth, no compromise
  › The Impression — at a price that lets you wear it every day
  › Both — show me the comparison

Step 6 — The Budget
[Budget range selector — EGP]
```

---

### The Result Page

```
"The Oracle has spoken."

[3–5 recommended products — mix of Originals and Impressions]
[Primary recommendation highlighted with reasoning in oracle's voice]

[Discovery Kit offer: "Try all three — EGP 250 · EGP 200 credit toward full bottle"]
[btn-primary: "Start with the discovery kit"]
[btn-secondary: "View the full collection"]

[Discount code: applied automatically on result page]

[Loyalty prompt if not logged in:
  "Save your oracle result — join The Crossing"]
```

---

### The Psychological Mechanics

**Commitment and consistency:** Answering questions about your preferences creates psychological investment in the result. You have told the system what you want — you are more likely to act on what it tells you back.

**Perceived personalization:** A recommendation generated "for you specifically" is perceived as more accurate, more trustworthy, and more worth acting on than a generic bestseller list — even when the underlying algorithm is identical.

**Reduced optionality:** The quiz collapses 48 choices to 3–5. Fewer choices at the point of decision produces higher conversion. This is one of the most robust findings in behavioral economics.

---

### The Performance Data

| **Metric**                                        | **Number**               |
| ------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------ |
| Quiz completion rate (well-designed tools)        | ~65% of those who start  |
| Conversion lift from quiz-based recommendation    | +30% during peak seasons |
| User satisfaction on match quality                | ~89%                     |
| Quiz start rate (of those who see it prominently) | ~40%                     |

---

### How Siwa Does It Differently

The quiz is not called a "Fragrance Finder." It is called **The Oracle.**

The questions are written in Siwa's voice — specific, intelligent, slightly apart from the expected. "Which landscape calls to you?" is a Siwa question. "What's your style?" is a generic e-commerce question.

The result page opens with "The Oracle has spoken." — not "Here are your matches!" The language maintains the mythology throughout the functional tool. The quiz is not a break from the brand experience. It is the brand experience.

The quiz result is saved to the customer's account. When they return, the Oracle remembers. This creates a longitudinal relationship — not a one-time interaction.

---

| **Component**                   | **Role**                                         |
| ------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------ |
| **`D-01 OracleQuiz`**           | Full quiz shell                                  |
| **`QuizStep`**                  | Question + choice grid per step                  |
| **`QuizChoice`**                | Selectable card — rest → gold border on selected |
| **`QuizProgress`**              | Step indicator with gold fill                    |

| **`E-07 LoyaltyPointsDisplay`** | Loyalty prompt for non-logged-in users           |

---

### Placement Rules

- **Primary navigation** — top-level destination, not buried
- **Homepage hero section** — one of the two primary CTAs
- **Homepage dedicated section** (Section 7) — with visual explanation of the flow
- **404 page** — the redirect for lost pilgrims
- **Cart empty state** — "Let the Oracle guide you"
- **Search empty state** — if no results found

---

### What Failure Looks Like

- Quiz buried in "Discover More" — not in primary navigation
- Generic questions that could apply to any product category
- Result page that does not include the tester option prominently
- No discount code or concrete incentive on the result page
- Quiz not mobile-optimized — 70%+ of traffic is on mobile
- Slow loading between steps — abandonment spikes above 3-second load times
- Result page that says "Here are your matches!" instead of "The Oracle has spoken."

---

### Phase

**P0 — Launch.** This is the store's primary conversion feature. It ships on Day 1.

## USF-02 — THE READING (PERSONAL SKIN DIAGNOSTIC)

**The oracle speaks specifically — not in general terms.**

---

### What It Is

A short personal diagnostic embedded on every PDP that outputs a verdict specific to the buyer's own skin and context — not the product's general profile.

The standard note pyramid stays as the objective baseline. The Reading sits below it as the personalized layer. Two pieces of information: what the product is, and what it will be _on you specifically._

---

### Trust Gaps Closed

| **Gap**   | **Role**  | **Mechanism**                                                                                          |
| --------- | --------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Gap 1** | Primary   | Transforms abstract note descriptions into a concrete prediction about a specific person's experience. |
| **Gap 3** | Primary   | Directly addresses the "will it last on my skin?" anxiety with skin-chemistry-aware output.            |
| **Gap 5** | Secondary | A personalized verdict makes the purchase feel considered and worth the money — not random.            |

---

### The Input (3–4 Questions, Maximum)

```
Question 1 — Skin type
  › Dry — fragrance fades faster, top notes shorter
  › Normal — behaves close to the pyramid description
  › Oily — fragrance amplifies and lasts longer

Question 2 — Climate / city
  › Cairo heat — heat accelerates development, compresses pyramid
  › Coastal humidity — projection extends, middle notes linger longer
  › Air-conditioned environment — behaves closest to lab conditions
  › Variable / other

Question 3 — Activity level
  › Desk / indoor — skin-close, intimate projection throughout
  › Outdoors / active — heat and movement amplify sillage significantly
  › Mixed

Question 4 — Which of these top 3 notes draws you most?
  [Populated dynamically from the specific product's note pyramid]
```

---

### The Output Format

A short, specific written verdict in the oracle's voice. Never generic. Never a template with placeholders visible.

**Format rule:**

_"[Skin trait] skin in [climate context]. Expect [specific note from this product] to arrive [faster/slower] than the pyramid suggests, and to outlast [other specific note] by about [honest timeframe estimate]. The base — [base note name] — is where this one lives on you."_

**Example output for a specific amber-oud composition:**

_"Oily skin in Cairo heat. The saffron will open faster than the pyramid suggests — give it ten minutes before you decide. The oud base, which takes two hours on cooler skin, arrives in under forty-five. This one will announce itself. Wear it for evenings, not offices."_

---

### Architectural Rules

**The standard note pyramid is never replaced.** The Reading is an additional layer — the pyramid is the objective product description, the Reading is the personalized prediction. Both are visible on every PDP.

**Data reuse:** The reading inputs are stored against the browser session and customer account. A repeat visitor does not re-answer. Every subsequent PDP shows their Reading automatically once they have completed it once. This turns a one-time interaction into a persistent personalization layer across the entire catalog.

**No account required:** The Reading is a lightweight micro-interaction. Lower commitment than the full Oracle quiz. It can feed customers toward the Oracle afterward — "Your Reading is based on your profile. For a full recommendation, ask the Oracle."

**The second CTA slot:** "Get your Reading" functions as a second call to action on every PDP without competing with "Add to bag" — it is informational, not transactional, so both can coexist above the fold.

---

### Why This Exists Nowhere Else

Competitor reviews are product-level. Star ratings and longevity notes describe how a fragrance performs in general — not how it performs on a specific person with specific skin chemistry in a specific climate.

The Reading makes every product page personalized to the individual. This is not a feature that any competitor has because it requires:

1. A structured skin-chemistry data model per customer
2. Note-level tagging per product
3. A brand voice capable of writing specific, non-generic output at scale
4. The mythology to frame it as a verdict rather than a disclaimer

Siwa has all four.

---

### The UGC Angle

Readings are inherently shareable. "Here is what the oracle said about how this wears on me" is a natural screenshot and story format — better UGC bait than a star rating. The specific, personal, oracle-voiced output is designed to be screenshotted and posted.

Content brief for community seeding: "Share your Reading."

---

### Components

| **Component**                     | **Role**                                                     |
| --------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **`ReadingInput`**                | 3–4 question micro-form on PDP                               |
| **`ReadingOutput`**               | Personalized verdict block, oracle voice, below note pyramid |
| **`P-04 NotePyramid`**            | The objective baseline — always present, never replaced      |
| **`P-06 LongevityProjectionBar`** | The general performance data — Reading personalizes it       |

---

### Placement Rules

- **Every PDP** — below the standard note pyramid, above the trust strip
- **Entry point:** "Get your Reading" as a secondary CTA alongside "Add to bag"
- **Persistent:** once completed, shows automatically on all subsequent PDPs without re-prompting
- **Oracle bridge:** Reading output includes a subtle link — "For a full recommendation across the catalog, ask the Oracle"

---

### What Failure Looks Like

- Generic output that could apply to any product — not this specific product's notes
- Output that uses placeholder language ("your note" instead of the actual note name)
- Requiring account creation before showing the Reading — too much friction
- Replacing the standard note pyramid instead of adding to it
- Not storing the inputs — making the customer re-answer on every PDP

---

### Phase

**P1 — Month 2–3.** Requires product-level note tagging infrastructure and the skin-chemistry output model. The Oracle quiz ships first (P0). The Reading builds on top of that foundation.

**The transparent ledger. The brand's most differentiated content format.**

---

### What It Is

Side-by-side comparison pages for every Original and its paired Impression — full note pyramid, longevity bars, projection bars, price, who each is for, and both tester CTAs. Neither option is presented as "better." Both are presented as different choices for different circumstances.

No competitor does this honestly. No competitor does it at all.

---

### Trust Gaps Closed

| **Gap**   | **Role**  | **Mechanism**                                                                                                                      |
| --------- | --------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Gap 1** | Primary   | Side-by-side note pyramid comparison is the deepest pre-purchase scent communication available in e-commerce.                      |
| **Gap 2** | Primary   | Transparency about what each product is and is not. The brand's honesty about its own model.                                       |
| **Gap 4** | Secondary | Both CTAs include the tester option — the comparison page is also a conversion surface.                                            |
| **Gap 6** | Secondary | A brand confident enough to put its impression next to the original it was inspired by — and not flinch — is a brand that is real. |

## USF-04 — THE SECOND CROSSING

**Every bottle carries a mark for what comes next.**

---

### What It Is

Every full-size bottle ships with a unique, single-use discount code printed on a card inside its package. The code activates automatically after the order is marked as delivered and can be redeemed on the customer's next purchase — with no requirement to return or mail back the physical bottle.

One bottle. One code. One next crossing.

The code is bound to the specific order and customer. It cannot be shared, reused, or activated before delivery confirmation. It is inert until the order is delivered.

---

### Trust Gaps Closed

| **Gap**   | **Role**  | **Mechanism**                                                                                                                         |
| --------- | --------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Gap 5** | Primary   | Extends the perceived value of every purchase beyond the liquid in the bottle. The bottle keeps giving after the transaction ends.    |
| **Gap 6** | Secondary | A brand that builds a ritual around every bottle — including what it earns for you next — is a brand that takes the object seriously. |

---

### The Mechanic in Full

```
1. Customer places an order containing one or more full-size bottles

2. For each full-size bottle in the order:
   › A unique coupon code is generated at order confirmation
   › Code status: PENDING

3. Order is marked DELIVERED by the logistics system

4. Code status changes: PENDING → ACTIVE
   › Customer receives an automated notification:
     Email / SMS: "Your bottle has earned its second crossing."
     [Code revealed] [CTA: "Use it on your next order"]

5. Customer applies the code at checkout on their next order
   › Code is validated against:
     [customer_id — correct customer only]
     [status: ACTIVE]
     [expiry: within 45 days of activation]
     [minimum order value: EGP 400]
     [non-stackable with other discount codes]

6. Code is redeemed:
   › Status changes: ACTIVE → REDEEMED
   › Code is permanently deactivated
   › Confirmation message: "The oracle remembers it."
```

---

### The Code Card — Physical Insert

Every full-size bottle package includes a printed card:

```
─────────────────────────────────────
THE SECOND CROSSING

This bottle carries a mark
for what comes next.

Your code: SIWA-7K4M-2Q9X

Valid on your next order only.
Single use. Expires in 45 days
from delivery.

The oracle remembers.
─────────────────────────────────────
```

Card dimensions: consistent with the package insert system. Printed on Parchment stock. Gold ink on the code itself.

---

### The Strategic Layer

**Impression codes are redeemable on Original purchases specifically.**

This is the deliberate trade-up mechanic. The customer who buys an Impression receives a code whose value is applied toward an Original — bridging the price gap and making the step up feel earned rather than expensive.

**Original codes are redeemable on any full-size purchase.**

This rewards the customer who has already committed at the highest level — and keeps them in the catalog without restriction.

| **Bottle Type**        | **Code Value** | **Redeemable On**       |
| ---------------------- | -------------- | ----------------------- |
| Impression (full-size) | EGP 75 off     | Original purchases only |
| Original (full-size)   | EGP 150 off    | Any full-size purchase  |
| Tester (85 EGP)        | No code issued | —                       |

---

### The Notification Sequence

```
Trigger: Order status → DELIVERED

Email subject: "Your bottle has earned its second crossing."

Body:
  "The [Product Name] has arrived.
   Inside the package, you will find a code — yours alone.

   It is valid for 45 days.
   It does not stack. It does not share.
   It opens one door.

   [CODE: SIWA-XXXX-XXXX]

   [btn-primary: Use it on your next order →]

   The oracle remembers."

---

Reminder at Day 30 (15 days before expiry):

Email subject: "Your second crossing closes in 15 days."

Body:
  "You have a code that has not been used yet.
   It expires on [date].

   [CODE: SIWA-XXXX-XXXX]

   [btn-primary: Use it before it closes →]"
```

---

### The Database Model

| **Field**             | **Description**                                                    |
| --------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| `coupon_code`         | Unique alphanumeric code — generated per bottle                    |
| `order_id`            | The order this code was generated from                             |
| `order_line_item_id`  | The specific bottle line item                                      |
| `customer_id`         | Bound to the purchasing customer only                              |
| `bottle_type`         | ORIGINAL / IMPRESSION — determines code value and redemption scope |
| `status`              | PENDING / ACTIVE / REDEEMED / EXPIRED                              |
| `generated_at`        | Timestamp of order confirmation                                    |
| `activated_at`        | Timestamp of delivery confirmation                                 |
| `expires_at`          | activated_at + 45 days                                             |
| `redeemed_at`         | Timestamp of redemption — null until redeemed                      |
| `discount_type`       | FIXED                                                              |
| `discount_value`      | EGP 75 (Impression) / EGP 150 (Original)                           |
| `redemption_scope`    | ORIGINALS_ONLY (Impression codes) / ALL_FULL_SIZE (Original codes) |
| `minimum_order_value` | EGP 400                                                            |
| `stackable`           | FALSE                                                              |

---

### Abuse Prevention Rules

- Code is bound to the `customer_id` of the original purchaser — cannot be used by a different account
- Code does not activate until the logistics system confirms delivery — not at order placement
- Code is single-use only — status moves to REDEEMED permanently on first use
- Code cannot be applied to the same order it was generated from
- Code cannot be stacked with any other active promotion or discount code
- Tester purchases do not generate codes — full-size only
- Expiry is enforced at the database level — not just the UI level

---

### The Framing Rule

**Do not use sustainability, recycling, or return language anywhere in this feature.** This is a repeat-purchase ritual — not an environmental initiative.

| **Do Not Say**                 | **Say Instead**                      |
| ------------------------------ | ------------------------------------ |
| "Your loyalty reward"          | "Your second crossing"               |
| "Discount for your next order" | "What this bottle earns"             |
| "Coupon code enclosed"         | "Inside, a mark for what comes next" |
| "Redeem your reward"           | "Open the second crossing"           |
| "Thank you for your purchase"  | "The oracle remembers."              |

- **Post-delivery email** — code revealed in the body of the notification

---

### What Failure Looks Like

- A generic discount code that any customer can type in — removes uniqueness, enables abuse
- Code activates at order placement instead of delivery — creates refund and cancellation exploits
- Code stackable with other discounts — margin erosion at scale
- Impression codes redeemable on Impressions — misses the trade-up mechanic entirely

## SF-09 — LIVE CARAVAN FEED

**Proof that other pilgrims are moving through the same crossing.**

**Phase: P1 — Month 2–3**

---

### What It Is

A live activity feed that surfaces real events happening across the Siwa store — tester purchases, delivered orders, Oracle completions, structured reviews, and Second Crossing activations — in Siwa's voice.

This is **not** fake urgency. It is not a randomized "12 people are viewing this" widget. Every line shown in the Caravan Feed must map to a real event in the database with a real timestamp and a real, generalized location.

If there is not enough real activity to justify the feed, it does not appear.

Silence is better than a lie.

---

### Trust Gaps Closed

| **Gap**   | **Role**  | **Mechanism**                                                                                                                    |
| --------- | --------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Gap 6** | Primary   | Proves the brand has real customers, real orders, and real movement — not just packaging and a polished site.                    |
| **Gap 5** | Secondary | Shared activity increases perceived legitimacy and value: other people are choosing, trying, and returning to Siwa in real time. |

---

### The Governing Rule

**Every number shown must be derived from an actual event.**

No seeded counts. No fake scarcity. No synthetic "currently viewing" messages. No ticker that runs 24/7 regardless of actual store activity.

The Caravan Feed exists only when reality justifies it.

---

### Eligible Event Types

The feed may draw from:

- Tester purchases
- Orders marked delivered
- Oracle quiz completions
- Structured review submissions
- Second Crossing code activations
- Original purchases by region
- Discovery Kit purchases
- Comparison-page conversions

All events must include:

- `event_type`
- `timestamp`
- `product_id` or `collection_context` where relevant
- generalized location (`city` / `governorate`, never precise address)
- display eligibility flag

---

### Message Examples

```
"3 pilgrims are trying [Product Name] in Cairo right now."

"A second crossing opened in Giza an hour ago."

"One seeker asked the Oracle about [Note Family] in the last ten minutes."

"12 people completed the Oracle today."

"The latest Original left for Alexandria 20 minutes ago."
```

**Tone rule:** numbers should feel plausible, not theatrical. Small, believable numbers are more trustworthy than inflated ones.

---

### Display Logic

The feed follows a strict hierarchy:

1. **Product-specific event** if enough recent product-level activity exists
2. **Collection-level event** if product-level activity is too low
3. **Sitewide event** if collection-level activity is too low
4. **Suppress entirely** if activity is below minimum threshold

This prevents the feed from forcing specificity where none exists.

---

### Suppression Rules

The feed must not render if:

- event volume is below the minimum threshold
- the only available events are stale
- the same message would repeat too often
- location data is missing or too precise to anonymize safely

Example suppression logic:

- no product-level message unless there are at least **2 eligible events** in the recent window
- no sitewide ticker unless there are at least **X eligible events** in the last **Y hours**
- repeated messages suppressed for a cooldown window

Exact thresholds should be calibrated against traffic reality — not ambition.

---

### Privacy Rules — Non-Negotiable

- No customer names
- No order numbers
- No exact addresses
- No phone numbers
- No event displayed instantly on occurrence

Every displayed event must have a **delay window** of at least **10–15 minutes** before becoming eligible. This prevents any event from being tied too directly to a real-world delivery or purchase a customer has just made.

Location must be generalized to city level: Cairo, Giza, Alexandria, Mansoura, etc. Never neighborhood. Never street.

---

### How It Uses Existing Infrastructure

The Live Caravan Feed does not require a new business model. It reuses systems already in the Bible:

- Oracle completions from USF-01
- Tester purchases from CCF-01
- Delivered-order status from USF-04 (The Second Crossing)
- Review submissions from CCF-06
- Product / collection context from the catalog model

It is a visibility layer over real activity already happening.

---

### Components

| **Component**                | **Role**                                                                                          |
| ---------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **`CaravanFeedTicker`**      | Frontend ticker / activity strip on homepage and PDP                                              |
| **`CaravanEventAggregator`** | Backend service that collects eligible events from orders, quizzes, reviews, and code activations |
| **`CaravanMessageRenderer`** | Converts raw events into oracle-voiced output                                                     |
| **`CaravanSuppressionRule`** | Prevents rendering when activity is too low or repetitive                                         |
| **`CaravanPrivacyFilter`**   | Strips precise data and enforces delay window before display                                      |

---

### Placement Rules

- **Homepage** — small strip below the hero, low drama, high credibility
- **PDP** — one-line live activity signal near the trust strip
- **Collection pages** — optional, only if event volume supports category-level messages
- **Never on cart**
- **Never on checkout**
- **Never as a modal or interruption**

This is a credibility layer, not a pressure tactic.

---

### What Failure Looks Like

- Fake or estimated numbers presented as real
- Feed appears even when no real activity exists
- Same number displayed all day
- Same line repeated every few minutes
- Precise personal data leaking into the message
- "Currently viewing" theater with no event source
- High-drama urgency language that breaks Siwa's tone
- Using the feed to pressure payment at checkout

---

### Phase

**P1 — Month 2–3.** Ship only after Siwa has enough real event volume for the feed to appear honestly. Do not force it on Day 1.

## SF-10 — ANNIVERSARY RE-SCENT

**The reminder that arrives before the bottle runs dry.**

**Phase: P1 pilot — Month 3 · P2 full rollout — Month 4–6**

---

### What It Is

A depletion-aware reorder reminder that predicts when a customer's bottle is likely to be nearing its end — and reaches out **before** that moment, not after.

Despite the name, this is **not** a calendar-anniversary campaign. The trigger is not "one month since purchase." The trigger is **predicted depletion**, based on bottle size, usage assumptions, Oracle profile data, and later, the customer's own purchase behavior.

The message always offers two paths:

1. **Continue the same journey** — reorder the same product
2. **Ask the Oracle what comes next** — discover a new recommendation

That keeps the feature from feeling like a blunt sales reminder.

---

### Trust Gaps Closed

| **Gap**   | **Role**  | **Mechanism**                                                                                                      |
| --------- | --------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Gap 5** | Primary   | Shows that the brand is following the customer's real journey with the product — not firing random promotions.     |
| **Gap 3** | Secondary | A thoughtful consumption estimate signals that Siwa understands how fragrance is actually worn and used over time. |

---

### Why This Exists

Most post-purchase flows are generic:

- "It's been 30 days."
- "Come back and shop again."
- "Here's 10% off."

Re-Scent is different. It aims to arrive at the moment the product is **likely** nearing completion — not according to a marketing calendar, but according to actual product behavior and customer context.

---

### The Inputs

The estimator may use:

- Bottle size (ml)
- Product usage model per SKU / bottle type
- Default ml-per-wear assumption
- Wear frequency inferred from Oracle Step 2 (every day, evening, travel, all occasions)
- Product category context (tester / full-size, Original / Impression)
- Order delivery date
- Repeat purchase behavior over time
- Optional account-level correction from customer input

---

### The Estimation Model

```
Estimated wears = bottle_volume_ml ÷ estimated_ml_per_wear

Expected depletion date =
  delivered_at + wear_frequency_model(estimated_wears, customer_profile)

Reminder send date =
  expected_depletion_date - 14 to 21 days
```

The model starts with defaults and improves over time through actual purchase behavior.

---

### The Timing Rule

The message should be sent **2–3 weeks before** the estimated depletion date.

Not after the bottle is likely empty. Not on the exact day of depletion. Not on a fixed monthly cycle.

This timing creates room to act without creating pressure.

---

### The Message Format

```
Subject:
"Your journey with [Product Name] is nearing its last crossing."

Body:
"Since [delivery month], [Product Name] has likely stayed with you more than once.

It looks as though the bottle may be nearing its final stretch.

If you want to continue the same journey, it is here.
If you want a different road, the Oracle can guide you.

[btn-primary: Continue this journey →]
[btn-secondary: Ask the Oracle what comes next →]"
```

---

### Second Crossing Integration

If the customer still has an **active Second Crossing code**, the reminder may surface it:

```
"You still have an open crossing:
[CODE: SIWA-XXXX-XXXX]"
```

If the code is expired, **do not mention it.**

No reminder should ever reference a benefit the customer can no longer use.

---

### Accuracy Rules

This feature must be framed as an estimate, not certainty.

Use language like:

- "It looks as though..."
- "It may be nearing..."
- "Likely in its final stretch..."

Do **not** say:

- "Your bottle is almost finished."
- "You must be out by now."
- "Your fragrance has run out."

The point is anticipation, not false precision.

---

### The Feedback Loop

The model must improve over time.

Signals that refine the estimate:

- customer reorders much earlier than predicted
- customer does not reorder long after the predicted depletion date
- customer buys the same fragrance repeatedly at a stable cadence
- customer updates account preferences: "I wear this more often" / "less often"

This creates a self-correcting system rather than a fixed rule.

---

### Account Layer

Inside the customer account, a section may show:

```
Active Bottles

[Product Name]
Likely nearing completion in: ~3 weeks
[btn: Reorder]
[btn: Ask the Oracle]

[Product Name]
Likely still early in its journey
```

This should be clearly labeled as an estimate.

---

### Eligibility Rules

Do not send Re-Scent reminders for:

- tester purchases
- canceled or refunded orders
- gift orders where the purchaser is not the wearer
- customers who opted out of behavioral reminders
- products without sufficient usage-model confidence

If confidence is too low, suppression is better than a bad guess.

---

### Components

| **Component**                 | **Role**                                             |
| ----------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------- |
| **`ConsumptionEstimator`**    | Backend model for expected depletion timing          |
| **`ProductUsageModel`**       | SKU-level usage assumptions per size and bottle type |
| **`ReScentNotification`**     | Email / SMS / in-account reminder trigger            |
| **`ConsumptionFeedbackLoop`** | Refines estimates using repurchase behavior          |
| **`Account — Dashboard`**     | Displays active-bottle estimate and reorder state    |
| **`PreferenceControl`**       | Lets customer reduce or disable these reminders      |

---

### Placement Rules

- **Email** — primary channel
- **SMS** — optional, opt-in only
- **Account dashboard** — "Active Bottles" / reorder estimation block
- **Never as a sitewide pop-up**
- **Never in checkout**
- **Never before the order is delivered**

This is post-purchase care, not acquisition pressure.

---

### What Failure Looks Like

- Reminder arrives while the bottle is obviously still very full
- Reminder arrives after the product is likely already finished
- Every customer receives the same timing regardless of profile
- No opt-out or preference control
- Message reads like a discount blast instead of a personal follow-up
- Model never improves after repeated customer behavior
- Reminder mentions an expired Second Crossing code
- Reminder sent for gift purchases or tester-only journeys

---

### Phase

**P1 — Month 3 pilot** for a limited cohort with conservative thresholds. **P2 — Month 4–6 full rollout** after the estimation model has been calibrated with real purchase history.

Do not roll this out broadly until the first feedback loop is in place.

**Maintenance rule:** Membership is **not permanent**. If the customer has not made a purchase for 6 months, they are gently moved outside the Circle — not expelled with drama, but they stop receiving new invitations and early access. This preserves the Circle's value as an active community, not a permanent label.

## SF-12 — NAME A COMPOSITION

**The fragrance is not just yours to wear — it is yours to name.**

**Phase: P2 — Month 4–6**

---

### What It Is

A structured contest tied to the launch of Siwa's first original composition. Instead of naming the fragrance internally, Siwa opens the naming to its most engaged customers — with guardrails that protect the brand's mythology.

This is not an open public vote. It is a curated submission process with internal final decision.

---

### Trust Gaps Closed

| **Gap**   | **Role**  | **Mechanism**                                                                                                                            |
| --------- | --------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Gap 6** | Primary   | A brand that involves its customers in creating the product itself — not just buying it — is the strongest proof that the brand is real. |
| **Gap 5** | Secondary | "I named that fragrance" creates emotional ownership far stronger than any discount or loyalty reward.                                   |

---

### Why This Is Different from Generic Naming Contests

The name must fit within Siwa's existing naming mythology (Oracle, Crossing, Reading, Compass, Mirage...). This constraint turns the contest itself into an education tool — customers must understand the brand's world to propose a name that belongs in it. The act of participating deepens their relationship with the mythology.

---

### The Contest Mechanic

```
1. Announcement:
   The new composition is described without a name:
   › Scent family, target feeling, the story behind its creation
   › "This composition needs a name. We are asking you."

2. Submission window (2 weeks):
   › Eligible participants only (minimum Crossings threshold, or Inner Circle members)
   › Each submission: proposed name + one line explaining why it fits
   › Maximum 1 submission per customer

3. Internal curation:
   › Siwa's team selects a shortlist of 5–10 names that genuinely fit the mythology
   › No open public vote on the full submission pool
     (Prevents "Boaty McBoatface" outcomes — this is a real, documented risk)

4. Optional community input:
   › The shortlist may be shared with Inner Circle members for a final vote
   › OR the team makes the final selection directly

5. Final decision:
   › Always in the team's hands — the community influences, but does not dictate
```

---

### The Winner's Reward

```
The winning namer receives:
  › Their name printed on every package for a limited period (3–6 months):
    "Named by [Name] — The Inner Circle"
  › A free bottle of the composition
  › Featured in the Field Notes article telling the composition's story
  › Bonus loyalty points / Crossings credit
```

**Cost to Siwa:** One free bottle + small label change on packaging for a limited run. The marketing and community value far exceeds the cost.

---

### The Content Value

- Every submission deepens the customer's engagement with Siwa's world
- The announcement of the winner is a content moment (video reveal, article, social)
- The limited-edition "Named by" packaging becomes a collectible / photographable item
- Participants who didn't win still invested in the brand's mythology — that investment doesn't disappear

---

### Components

| **Component**                     | **Role**                                                       |
| --------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **`CompositionNamingSubmission`** | Submission form: proposed name + one-line rationale            |
| **`ShortlistCurationPanel`**      | Internal tool for the team to filter and select the shortlist  |
| **`WinnerAnnouncementPage`**      | Public page revealing the winner and the story behind the name |
| **`LimitedPackagingLabel`**       | "Named by [Name]" label design for limited production run      |

---

### Relationship with Other Systems

- **The Inner Circle (SF-11):** The naming contest can be restricted to Inner Circle members — making it an exclusive privilege that adds weight to both features.
- **Field Notes (SF-05):** The composition's launch article naturally includes the naming story.
- **TikTok Shop (SF-08):** The naming reveal is a natural content format — a video moment.

---

### What Failure Looks Like

- Fully open public vote with no internal curation — risk of inappropriate or off-brand results
- A name that doesn't fit the existing mythology — breaks the naming system
- Failing to actually use the name — asking for input and then ignoring it destroys trust permanently
- No content capture around the winner announcement — wasted marketing opportunity
- Contest open to everyone with no engagement threshold — devalues the "our real customers chose this" framing

---

### Phase

**P2 — Month 4–6.** Directly tied to the launch of Siwa's first original composition. Cannot happen before that composition exists. Ideally paired with the Inner Circle launch so the contest serves as the Circle's first concrete, visible privilege.

USF-02 — THE READING (PERSONAL SKIN DIAGNOSTIC)
The oracle speaks specifically — not in general terms.

The Distinction From The Oracle — Why Both Exist
This is the question that must be answered clearly, because on the surface they look similar: both involve questions, both produce personalized output.

They are fundamentally different in three ways:

Dimension The Oracle (USF-01) The Reading (USF-02)
Scope Across the entire catalog — "which fragrance is right for you?" On one specific product — "what will this fragrance do on you?"
Timing Pre-browsing — helps the customer decide where to start Mid-consideration — helps the customer decide whether to commit
Output A shortlist of 3–5 products A single verdict about one specific product on one specific skin
Questions Preferences, occasions, budget, landscape Skin type, local climate, activity level, note preference
Commitment level Larger — 6 questions, a full consultation Lighter — 3–4 questions, a quick check
Position in funnel Top of funnel — discovery Middle of funnel — consideration/conversion
The Oracle answers: "What should I explore?"

The Reading answers: "Should I buy this specific one — and what will it actually be like on me?"

They are two different questions at two different moments in the customer journey. One does not replace the other.

What It Is
A short personal diagnostic embedded on every PDP that outputs a verdict specific to the buyer's own skin and context — not the product's general profile.

The standard note pyramid stays as the objective baseline. The Reading sits below it as the personalized layer.

Trust Gaps Closed
Gap Role Mechanism
Gap 1 Primary Transforms abstract note descriptions into a concrete prediction about a specific person's experience.
Gap 3 Primary Directly addresses the "will it last on my skin?" anxiety with skin-chemistry-aware output.
Gap 5 Secondary A personalized verdict makes the purchase feel considered and worth the money.
The Input (3–4 Questions, Maximum)

Dry — fragrance fades faster, top notes shorter
Normal — behaves close to the pyramid description
Oily — fragrance amplifies and lasts longer
Question 2 — Climate / city

Cairo heat — heat accelerates development, compresses pyramid
Coastal humidity — projection extends, middle notes linger longer
Air-conditioned environment — behaves closest to lab conditions
Variable / other
Question 3 — Activity level

Desk / indoor — skin-close, intimate projection throughout

Mixed
Question 4 — Which of these top 3 notes draws you most?
(Populated dynamically from the specific product's note pyramid)

The Output Format
A short, specific written verdict in the oracle's voice. Never generic. Never a template with placeholders visible.

Format rule:

"[Skin trait] skin in [climate context]. Expect [specific note from this product] to arrive [faster/slower] than the pyramid suggests, and to outlast [other specific note] by about [honest timeframe estimate]. The base — [base note name] — is where this one lives on you."

Example output for an amber-oud composition:

"Oily skin in Cairo heat. The saffron will open faster than the pyramid suggests — give it ten minutes before you decide. The oud base, which takes two hours on cooler skin, arrives in under forty-five. This one will announce itself. Wear it for evenings, not offices."

USF-04 — THE SECOND CROSSING
Every bottle carries a mark for what comes next.

What It Is
Every full-size bottle ships with a unique, single-use discount code printed on a card inside its package. The code activates automatically after the order is marked as delivered and can be redeemed on the customer's next purchase.

One bottle. One code. One next crossing.

Trust Gaps Closed

Gap 5 Primary Extends the perceived value of every purchase beyond the liquid in the bottle.

The Mechanic in Full
Customer places an order containing one or more full-size bottles
For each full-size bottle: a unique coupon code is generated at order confirmation · Code status: PENDING
Order is marked DELIVERED by the logistics system
Code status changes: PENDING → ACTIVE
Customer receives automated notification: "Your bottle has earned its second crossing."
Customer applies the code at checkout on their next order
Validated against: correct customer ID · ACTIVE status · 45-day expiry · EGP 400 minimum · non-stackable
Code is redeemed → status changes: ACTIVE → REDEEMED → permanently deactivated

Bottle Type Code Value Redeemable On

Original (full-size) EGP 150 off Any full-size purchase

## USF-02 — THE READING (PERSONAL SKIN DIAGNOSTIC)

**The oracle speaks specifically — not in general terms.**

---

### What It Is

A short personal diagnostic embedded on every PDP that outputs a verdict specific to the buyer's own skin and context — not the product's general profile.

The standard note pyramid stays as the objective baseline. The Reading sits below it as the personalized layer. Two pieces of information: what the product is, and what it will be _on you specifically._

---

### Trust Gaps Closed

| **Gap**   | **Role**  | **Mechanism**                                                                                          |
| --------- | --------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Gap 1** | Primary   | Transforms abstract note descriptions into a concrete prediction about a specific person's experience. |
| **Gap 3** | Primary   | Directly addresses the "will it last on my skin?" anxiety with skin-chemistry-aware output.            |
| **Gap 5** | Secondary | A personalized verdict makes the purchase feel considered and worth the money — not random.            |

---

### The Input (3–4 Questions, Maximum)

```
Question 1 — Skin type
  › Dry — fragrance fades faster, top notes shorter
  › Normal — behaves close to the pyramid description
  › Oily — fragrance amplifies and lasts longer

Question 2 — Climate / city
  › Cairo heat — heat accelerates development, compresses pyramid
  › Coastal humidity — projection extends, middle notes linger longer
  › Air-conditioned environment — behaves closest to lab conditions
  › Variable / other

Question 3 — Activity level
  › Desk / indoor — skin-close, intimate projection throughout
  › Outdoors / active — heat and movement amplify sillage significantly
  › Mixed

Question 4 — Which of these top 3 notes draws you most?
  [Populated dynamically from the specific product's note pyramid]
```

---

### The Output Format

A short, specific written verdict in the oracle's voice. Never generic. Never a template with placeholders visible.

**Format rule:**

_"[Skin trait] skin in [climate context]. Expect [specific note from this product] to arrive [faster/slower] than the pyramid suggests, and to outlast [other specific note] by about [honest timeframe estimate]. The base — [base note name] — is where this one lives on you."_

**Example output for a specific amber-oud composition:**

_"Oily skin in Cairo heat. The saffron will open faster than the pyramid suggests — give it ten minutes before you decide. The oud base, which takes two hours on cooler skin, arrives in under forty-five. This one will announce itself. Wear it for evenings, not offices."_

---

### Architectural Rules

**The standard note pyramid is never replaced.** The Reading is an additional layer — the pyramid is the objective product description, the Reading is the personalized prediction. Both are visible on every PDP.

**Data reuse:** The reading inputs are stored against the browser session and customer account. A repeat visitor does not re-answer. Every subsequent PDP shows their Reading automatically once they have completed it once. This turns a one-time interaction into a persistent personalization layer across the entire catalog.

**No account required:** The Reading is a lightweight micro-interaction. Lower commitment than the full Oracle quiz. It can feed customers toward the Oracle afterward — "Your Reading is based on your profile. For a full recommendation, ask the Oracle."

**The second CTA slot:** "Get your Reading" functions as a second call to action on every PDP without competing with "Add to bag" — it is informational, not transactional, so both can coexist above the fold.

---

### Why This Exists Nowhere Else

Competitor reviews are product-level. Star ratings and longevity notes describe how a fragrance performs in general — not how it performs on a specific person with specific skin chemistry in a specific climate.

The Reading makes every product page personalized to the individual. This is not a feature that any competitor has because it requires:

1. A structured skin-chemistry data model per customer
2. Note-level tagging per product
3. A brand voice capable of writing specific, non-generic output at scale
4. The mythology to frame it as a verdict rather than a disclaimer

Siwa has all four.

---

### The UGC Angle

Readings are inherently shareable. "Here is what the oracle said about how this wears on me" is a natural screenshot and story format — better UGC bait than a star rating. The specific, personal, oracle-voiced output is designed to be screenshotted and posted.

Content brief for community seeding: "Share your Reading."

## USF-04 — THE SECOND CROSSING

**Every bottle carries a mark for what comes next.**

---

### What It Is

Every full-size bottle ships with a unique, single-use discount code printed on a card inside its package. The code activates automatically after the order is marked as delivered and can be redeemed on the customer's next purchase — with no requirement to return or mail back the physical bottle.

One bottle. One code. One next crossing.

The code is bound to the specific order and customer. It cannot be shared, reused, or activated before delivery confirmation. It is inert until the order is delivered.

---

### Trust Gaps Closed

| **Gap**   | **Role**  | **Mechanism**                                                                                                                         |
| --------- | --------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Gap 5** | Primary   | Extends the perceived value of every purchase beyond the liquid in the bottle. The bottle keeps giving after the transaction ends.    |
| **Gap 6** | Secondary | A brand that builds a ritual around every bottle — including what it earns for you next — is a brand that takes the object seriously. |

---

### The Mechanic in Full

```
1. Customer places an order containing one or more full-size bottles

2. For each full-size bottle in the order:
   › A unique coupon code is generated at order confirmation
   › Code status: PENDING

3. Order is marked DELIVERED by the logistics system

4. Code status changes: PENDING → ACTIVE
   › Customer receives an automated notification:
     Email / SMS: "Your bottle has earned its second crossing."
     [Code revealed] [CTA: "Use it on your next order"]

5. Customer applies the code at checkout on their next order
   › Code is validated against:
     [customer_id — correct customer only]
     [status: ACTIVE]
     [expiry: within 45 days of activation]
     [minimum order value: EGP 400]
     [non-stackable with other discount codes]

6. Code is redeemed:
   › Status changes: ACTIVE → REDEEMED
   › Code is permanently deactivated
   › Confirmation message: "The oracle remembers it."
```

---

### The Code Card — Physical Insert

Every full-size bottle package includes a printed card:

```

```

**Impression codes are redeemable on Original purchases specifically.**

This is the deliberate trade-up mechanic. The customer who buys an Impression receives a code whose value is applied toward an Original — bridging the price gap and making the step up feel earned rather than expensive.

**Original codes are redeemable on any full-size purchase.**

This rewards the customer who has already committed at the highest level — and keeps them in the catalog without restriction.

| `order_line_item_id`  | The specific bottle line item                                      |
| `customer_id`         | Bound to the purchasing customer only                              |
| `bottle_type`         | ORIGINAL / IMPRESSION — determines code value and redemption scope |
| `status`              | PENDING / ACTIVE / REDEEMED / EXPIRED                              |
| `generated_at`        | Timestamp of order confirmation                                    |
| `activated_at`        | Timestamp of delivery confirmation                                 |
| `expires_at`          | activated_at + 45 days                                             |
| `redeemed_at`         | Timestamp of redemption — null until redeemed                      |
| `discount_type`       | FIXED                                                              |
| `discount_value`      | EGP 75 (Impression) / EGP 150 (Original)                           |
| `redemption_scope`    | ORIGINALS_ONLY (Impression codes) / ALL_FULL_SIZE (Original codes) |
| `minimum_order_value` | EGP 400                                                            |
| `stackable`           | FALSE                                                              |

---

### Abuse Prevention Rules

- Code is bound to the `customer_id` of the original purchaser — cannot be used by a different account
- Code does not activate until the logistics system confirms delivery — not at order placement
- Code is single-use only — status moves to REDEEMED permanently on first use
- Code cannot be applied to the same order it was generated from
- Code cannot be stacked with any other active promotion or discount code
- Tester purchases do not generate codes — full-size only
- Expiry is enforced at the database level — not just the UI level

## SF-09 — LIVE CARAVAN FEED

**Proof that other pilgrims are moving through the same crossing.**

**Phase: P1 — Month 2–3**

---

### What It Is

A live activity feed that surfaces real events happening across the Siwa store — tester purchases, delivered orders, Oracle completions, structured reviews, and Second Crossing activations — in Siwa's voice.

This is **not** fake urgency. It is not a randomized "12 people are viewing this" widget. Every line shown in the Caravan Feed must map to a real event in the database with a real timestamp and a real, generalized location.

If there is not enough real activity to justify the feed, it does not appear.

Silence is better than a lie.

---

### Trust Gaps Closed

| **Gap**   | **Role**  | **Mechanism**                                                                                                                    |
| --------- | --------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Gap 6** | Primary   | Proves the brand has real customers, real orders, and real movement — not just packaging and a polished site.                    |
| **Gap 5** | Secondary | Shared activity increases perceived legitimacy and value: other people are choosing, trying, and returning to Siwa in real time. |

---

### The Governing Rule

**Every number shown must be derived from an actual event.**

No seeded counts. No fake scarcity. No synthetic "currently viewing" messages. No ticker that runs 24/7 regardless of actual store activity.

The Caravan Feed exists only when reality justifies it.

---

### Eligible Event Types

The feed may draw from:

- Tester purchases
- Orders marked delivered
- Oracle quiz completions
- Structured review submissions
- Second Crossing code activations
- Original purchases by region
- Discovery Kit purchases
- Comparison-page conversions

All events must include:

- `event_type`
- `timestamp`
- `product_id` or `collection_context` where relevant
- generalized location (`city` / `governorate`, never precise address)
- display eligibility flag

---

### Message Examples

```
"3 pilgrims are trying [Product Name] in Cairo right now."

"A second crossing opened in Giza an hour ago."

"One seeker asked the Oracle about [Note Family] in the last ten minutes."

"12 people completed the Oracle today."

"The latest Original left for Alexandria 20 minutes ago."
```

**Tone rule:** numbers should feel plausible, not theatrical. Small, believable numbers are more trustworthy than inflated ones.

---

### Display Logic

The feed follows a strict hierarchy:

1. **Product-specific event** if enough recent product-level activity exists
2. **Collection-level event** if product-level activity is too low
3. **Sitewide event** if collection-level activity is too low
4. **Suppress entirely** if activity is below minimum threshold

This prevents the feed from forcing specificity where none exists.

---

### Suppression Rules

The feed must not render if:

- event volume is below the minimum threshold
- the only available events are stale
- the same message would repeat too often
- location data is missing or too precise to anonymize safely

Example suppression logic:

- no product-level message unless there are at least **2 eligible events** in the recent window
- no sitewide ticker unless there are at least **X eligible events** in the last **Y hours**
- repeated messages suppressed for a cooldown window

Exact thresholds should be calibrated against traffic reality — not ambition.

---

### Privacy Rules — Non-Negotiable

- No customer names
- No order numbers
- No exact addresses
- No phone numbers
- No event displayed instantly on occurrence

Every displayed event must have a **delay window** of at least **10–15 minutes** before becoming eligible. This prevents any event from being tied too directly to a real-world delivery or purchase a customer has just made.

Location must be generalized to city level: Cairo, Giza, Alexandria, Mansoura, etc. Never neighborhood. Never street.

---

### How It Uses Existing Infrastructure

The Live Caravan Feed does not require a new business model. It reuses systems already in the Bible:

- Oracle completions from USF-01
- Tester purchases from CCF-01
- Delivered-order status from USF-04 (The Second Crossing)
- Review submissions from CCF-06
- Product / collection context from the catalog model

It is a visibility layer over real activity already happening.

### Components

| ---------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **`CaravanFeedTicker`**      | Frontend ticker / activity strip on homepage and PDP                                              |
| **`CaravanEventAggregator`** | Backend service that collects eligible events from orders, quizzes, reviews, and code activations |

This is a credibility layer, not a pressure tactic.

## SF-10 — ANNIVERSARY RE-SCENT

**The reminder that arrives before the bottle runs dry.**

**Phase: P1 pilot — Month 3 · P2 full rollout — Month 4–6**

---

### What It Is

A depletion-aware reorder reminder that predicts when a customer's bottle is likely to be nearing its end — and reaches out **before** that moment, not after.

Despite the name, this is **not** a calendar-anniversary campaign. The trigger is not "one month since purchase." The trigger is **predicted depletion**, based on bottle size, usage assumptions, Oracle profile data, and later, the customer's own purchase behavior.

The message always offers two paths:

1. **Continue the same journey** — reorder the same product
2. **Ask the Oracle what comes next** — discover a new recommendation

That keeps the feature from feeling like a blunt sales reminder.

---

### Trust Gaps Closed

| **Gap**   | **Role**  | **Mechanism**                                                                                                      |
| --------- | --------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Gap 5** | Primary   | Shows that the brand is following the customer's real journey with the product — not firing random promotions.     |
| **Gap 3** | Secondary | A thoughtful consumption estimate signals that Siwa understands how fragrance is actually worn and used over time. |

---

### Why This Exists

Most post-purchase flows are generic:

- "It's been 30 days."
- "Come back and shop again."
- "Here's 10% off."

Re-Scent is different. It aims to arrive at the moment the product is **likely** nearing completion — not according to a marketing calendar, but according to actual product behavior and customer context.

---

### The Inputs

The estimator may use:

- Bottle size (ml)
- Product usage model per SKU / bottle type
- Default ml-per-wear assumption
- Wear frequency inferred from Oracle Step 2 (every day, evening, travel, all occasions)
- Product category context (tester / full-size, Original / Impression)
- Order delivery date
- Repeat purchase behavior over time
- Optional account-level correction from customer input

---

### The Estimation Model

```
Estimated wears = bottle_volume_ml ÷ estimated_ml_per_wear

Expected depletion date =
  delivered_at + wear_frequency_model(estimated_wears, customer_profile)

Reminder send date =
  expected_depletion_date - 14 to 21 days
```

The model starts with defaults and improves over time through actual purchase behavior.

---

### The Timing Rule

The message should be sent **2–3 weeks before** the estimated depletion date.

Not after the bottle is likely empty. Not on the exact day of depletion. Not on a fixed monthly cycle.

This timing creates room to act without creating pressure.

---

### The Message Format

```
Subject:
"Your journey with [Product Name] is nearing its last crossing."

Body:
"Since [delivery month], [Product Name] has likely stayed with you more than once.

It looks as though the bottle may be nearing its final stretch.

If you want to continue the same journey, it is here.
If you want a different road, the Oracle can guide you.

[btn-primary: Continue this journey →]
[btn-secondary: Ask the Oracle what comes next →]"
```

---

### Second Crossing Integration

If the customer still has an **active Second Crossing code**, the reminder may surface it:

```
"You still have an open crossing:
[CODE: SIWA-XXXX-XXXX]"
```

If the code is expired, **do not mention it.**

No reminder should ever reference a benefit the customer can no longer use.

---

### Accuracy Rules

This feature must be framed as an estimate, not certainty.

Use language like:

- "It looks as though..."
- "It may be nearing..."
- "Likely in its final stretch..."

Do **not** say:

- "Your bottle is almost finished."
- "You must be out by now."
- "Your fragrance has run out."

The point is anticipation, not false precision.

---

### The Feedback Loop

The model must improve over time.

Signals that refine the estimate:

- customer reorders much earlier than predicted
- customer does not reorder long after the predicted depletion date
- customer buys the same fragrance repeatedly at a stable cadence
- customer updates account preferences: "I wear this more often" / "less often"

This creates a self-correcting system rather than a fixed rule.

---

### Account Layer

Inside the customer account, a section may show:

```
Active Bottles

[Product Name]
Likely nearing completion in: ~3 weeks
[btn: Reorder]
[btn: Ask the Oracle]

[Product Name]
Likely still early in its journey
```

This should be clearly labeled as an estimate.

---

### Eligibility Rules

Do not send Re-Scent reminders for:

- tester purchases
- canceled or refunded orders
- gift orders where the purchaser is not the wearer
- customers who opted out of behavioral reminders
- products without sufficient usage-model confidence

If confidence is too low, suppression is better than a bad guess.

---

### Components

| **Component**                 | **Role**                                             |
| ----------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------- |
| **`ConsumptionEstimator`**    | Backend model for expected depletion timing          |
| **`ProductUsageModel`**       | SKU-level usage assumptions per size and bottle type |
| **`ReScentNotification`**     | Email / SMS / in-account reminder trigger            |
| **`ConsumptionFeedbackLoop`** | Refines estimates using repurchase behavior          |
| **`Account — Dashboard`**     | Displays active-bottle estimate and reorder state    |
| **`PreferenceControl`**       | Lets customer reduce or disable these reminders      |

---

### Placement Rules

- **Email** — primary channel
- **SMS** — optional, opt-in only
- **Account dashboard** — "Active Bottles" / reorder estimation block
- **Never as a sitewide pop-up**
- **Never in checkout**
- **Never before the order is delivered**

This is post-purchase care, not acquisition pressure.

---

### What Failure Looks Like

- Reminder arrives while the bottle is obviously still very full
- Reminder arrives after the product is likely already finished
- Every customer receives the same timing regardless of profile
- No opt-out or preference control
- Message reads like a discount blast instead of a personal follow-up
- Model never improves after repeated customer behavior
- Reminder mentions an expired Second Crossing code
- Reminder sent for gift purchases or tester-only journeys

---

### Phase

**P1 — Month 3 pilot** for a limited cohort with conservative thresholds. **P2 — Month 4–6 full rollout** after the estimation model has been calibrated with real purchase history.

Do not roll this out broadly until the first feedback loop is in place.

## SF-12 — NAME A COMPOSITION

**The fragrance is not just yours to wear — it is yours to name.**

**Phase: P2 — Month 4–6**

---

### What It Is

A structured contest tied to the launch of Siwa's first original composition. Instead of naming the fragrance internally, Siwa opens the naming to its most engaged customers — with guardrails that protect the brand's mythology.

This is not an open public vote. It is a curated submission process with internal final decision.

---

### Trust Gaps Closed

| **Gap**   | **Role**  | **Mechanism**                                                                                                                            |
| --------- | --------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Gap 6** | Primary   | A brand that involves its customers in creating the product itself — not just buying it — is the strongest proof that the brand is real. |
| **Gap 5** | Secondary | "I named that fragrance" creates emotional ownership far stronger than any discount or loyalty reward.                                   |

---

### Why This Is Different from Generic Naming Contests

The name must fit within Siwa's existing naming mythology (Oracle, Crossing, Reading, Compass, Mirage...). This constraint turns the contest itself into an education tool — customers must understand the brand's world to propose a name that belongs in it. The act of participating deepens their relationship with the mythology.

---

### The Contest Mechanic

```
1. Announcement:
   The new composition is described without a name:
   › Scent family, target feeling, the story behind its creation
   › "This composition needs a name. We are asking you."

2. Submission window (2 weeks):
   › Eligible participants only (minimum Crossings threshold, or Inner Circle members)
   › Each submission: proposed name + one line explaining why it fits
   › Maximum 1 submission per customer

3. Internal curation:
   › Siwa's team selects a shortlist of 5–10 names that genuinely fit the mythology
   › No open public vote on the full submission pool

USF-02 — THE READING (PERSONAL SKIN DIAGNOSTIC)
The oracle speaks specifically — not in general terms.

The Distinction From The Oracle — Why Both Exist
This is the question that must be answered clearly, because on the surface they look similar: both involve questions, both produce personalized output.

They are fundamentally different in three ways:

Dimension The Oracle (USF-01) The Reading (USF-02)
Scope Across the entire catalog — "which fragrance is right for you?" On one specific product — "what will this fragrance do on you?"
Timing Pre-browsing — helps the customer decide where to start Mid-consideration — helps the customer decide whether to commit
Output A shortlist of 3–5 products A single verdict about one specific product on one specific skin
Questions Preferences, occasions, budget, landscape Skin type, local climate, activity level, note preference
Commitment level Larger — 6 questions, a full consultation Lighter — 3–4 questions, a quick check
Position in funnel Top of funnel — discovery Middle of funnel — consideration/conversion
The Oracle answers: "What should I explore?"

The Reading answers: "Should I buy this specific one — and what will it actually be like on me?"

They are two different questions at two different moments in the customer journey. One does not replace the other.

What It Is
A short personal diagnostic embedded on every PDP that outputs a verdict specific to the buyer's own skin and context — not the product's general profile.

The standard note pyramid stays as the objective baseline. The Reading sits below it as the personalized layer.

Trust Gaps Closed
Gap Role Mechanism
Gap 1 Primary Transforms abstract note descriptions into a concrete prediction about a specific person's experience.
Gap 3 Primary Directly addresses the "will it last on my skin?" anxiety with skin-chemistry-aware output.
Gap 5 Secondary A personalized verdict makes the purchase feel considered and worth the money.
The Input (3–4 Questions, Maximum)

Dry — fragrance fades faster, top notes shorter
Normal — behaves close to the pyramid description
Oily — fragrance amplifies and lasts longer
Question 2 — Climate / city

Cairo heat — heat accelerates development, compresses pyramid
Coastal humidity — projection extends, middle notes linger longer
Air-conditioned environment — behaves closest to lab conditions
Variable / other
Question 3 — Activity level

Desk / indoor — skin-close, intimate projection throughout

Mixed
Question 4 — Which of these top 3 notes draws you most?
(Populated dynamically from the specific product's note pyramid)

The Output Format
A short, specific written verdict in the oracle's voice. Never generic. Never a template with placeholders visible.

Format rule:

"[Skin trait] skin in [climate context]. Expect [specific note from this product] to arrive [faster/slower] than the pyramid suggests, and to outlast [other specific note] by about [honest timeframe estimate]. The base — [base note name] — is where this one lives on you."

"Oily skin in Cairo heat. The saffron will open faster than the pyramid suggests — give it ten minutes before you decide. The oud base, which takes two hours on cooler skin, arrives in under forty-five. This one will announce itself. Wear it for evenings, not offices."

Why This Is a Trust Gap
A slow website in Egypt is not just a bad experience — it is a direct cause of abandonment and distrust. If the Oracle quiz takes 5 seconds to load between steps, customers leave. If the PDP images load slowly, the premium unboxing experience fails before the order is placed.
