01How to read this document

Each feature below carries the same four lines. The third and fourth are the ones that matter: nothing is on this list because a competitor has it — it is here because a named reference proved the pattern and Siwa has a measured gap it closes.

Inspired by

The house the pattern is taken from, with the section of Planning/DIRECTION.md or BENCHMARK.md that records the decision.

Built

Exists in the 78-component library, rendered against the real 56-product catalogue.

Specified

Decided and documented, not yet built — or built with placeholder data pending a migration.

Blocked

Waiting on a decision that is commercial or legal, not technical. Listed openly in §6.

The evidence rule

Every Siwa figure quoted here comes from the July 2026 full-catalogue capture and its authoritative corrections file; every competitor pattern was read from the live site during the benchmark sessions. No conversion-uplift percentages appear in this document. Uplift depends on traffic, price and season, and any number quoted before the feature ships would be invented. What is stated instead is the mechanism — the funnel step the feature acts on — and the measured Siwa gap that mechanism addresses.

The firewall the roster runs on

Identity references (Amouage, Widian, Kahina, Fueguia) govern voice, story, palette, typography, naming and architecture. Snif governs commerce mechanics. Oakcha and Skylar are tactical only — they determine what a module contains and how it behaves, never how it sounds or looks. A decision that would change a word a customer reads, a colour or a product name is out of scope for the tactical pair, however well they do it.

One reference is deliberately rejected

Amouage's product cards carry no reviews and no ratings — restraint a house selling at $395 can afford. 03The feature programme

Six tracks, ordered by where they sit in the funnel: findability first, then the product page, then basket size, then trust, then identity, then return visits.

Track A

Findability — getting the customer to the right bottle

Today the store has ten flat tag-driven links and no filtering of any kind. Around 35% of the catalogue cannot be reached by browsing at all.

Faceted collection filtering

A-01
BuiltOakcha

Eight filter axes — scent family, gender, concentration, size, price, availability, collection, and the differentiated one: the designer house a fragrance is inspired by — each rendering a live product count, in a sidebar on desktop and a drawer on mobile.

Inspired by

Oakcha — 80+ scent-family values and a 28-house brand facet, every value carrying a count. DIRECTION.md Part 2 §3.2B

Not copied

Oakcha's compound families (Fruity–Gourmand–Sweet) are a data-hygiene failure and unusable at 56 SKUs. We use a flat Fragrantica vocabulary and let a product carry several values.

Why it converts

Filtering is the cheapest way to shorten the path from landing to product page. The designer facet does something stronger: it converts search intent the store already receives — "something like Baccarat Rouge" — into a browsable route with an answer at the end of it.

Siwa's gap

17 products have an empty tags array and 21 carry no gender tag, including 9 of the 10 published in 2026. Because the nav is tag-driven, those products are currently unreachable except by direct link.

Scent quiz → Persona

A-02
BuiltScoring pendingSkylarOkhtein

A short multi-step quiz that resolves to one of five personas — Goddess, Poet, Muse, Heir, Nomad — and returns a primary fragrance plus a suggested layering partner, both addable to the bag in one action at the existing bundle price.

Inspired by

Skylar for the architecture and, uniquely, for returning a combination rather than a single product. Okhtein — the Egyptian house — for archetype-named collections. DIRECTION.md Part 2 §4.2A · BENCHMARK.md Part 2 §3C

Not copied

Skylar's naming. "Core Scents" is their voice; the persona names are Siwa's own and belong to the identity track.

Why it converts

Fragrance is the hardest category to buy unseen, and a quiz replaces an unassisted 56-product grid with a two-item recommendation. It also raises order value by construction, because the output is a pair rather than a bottle.

Siwa's gap

The announcement bar already reads "Explore your Persona" and five products use "Persona" in their copy — the store's most-repeated line currently points at nothing. Every Tier 1 and Tier 2 comparable has a quiz; Siwa has none.

Dual-track navigation and narrative collections

A-03
BuiltAmouageFueguia

A header that splits commerce (Perfumes · Collections · Body · Discovery) from a separate House of Siwa story track, and collections renamed narratively — The Oasis Collection, The Salt Collection — instead of perfumes and new-drops.

Inspired by

Amouage for the two-track split and the serialised, narratively-named collections; Fueguia for the four-pillar naming used inside the story track. DIRECTION.md Part 1 §2.1, §2.4

Not copied

Fueguia's ordering. Fueguia deliberately puts editorial before commerce; that suits a house selling rare compositions to collectors and would bury a 56-SKU store that needs to convert. We take the naming, not the hierarchy.

Why it converts

Renaming a collection costs nothing and changes what a shopper thinks they are browsing. Separating the story track keeps brand content discoverable without pushing it in front of the buy path.

Siwa's gap

Nine of 19 collections, two of five pages and the entire blog are navigation orphans — including /pages/our-story, which is linked from nothing.

Predictive search

A-04
BuiltAPI pending

A search overlay with as-you-type product results, collection suggestions and empty-state recovery, working in both scripts.

Inspired by

Baseline commerce hygiene rather than a specific house — present across the whole benchmark set.

Why it converts

Searchers are the highest-intent traffic on any store. With a broken tag-driven nav, search is currently the only reliable route to about a third of the catalogue.

Status

The library version matches client-side over the embedded catalogue. The live build needs Shopify's predictive-search API behind it.

Sort, pagination, breadcrumbs, recently viewed

A-05
Built

Five sort orders including highest rated, numbered pagination, RTL-aware breadcrumbs, and a recently-viewed rail persisted locally.

Inspired by

Oakcha ships nine sort options; the sort set here is trimmed to what 56 products can meaningfully support.

Why it converts

Highest rated is the sort that matters for this store specifically: it surfaces a 4.98★ corpus as a browse axis instead of leaving it buried on individual product pages. Recently-viewed recovers the return visitor who left mid-comparison.

Track B

The product page — answering the questions that stop a purchase

The entire content model currently lives in body_html, across five incompatible description formats. Only 18 of 56 products expose notes a machine can read, using 13 different tier-label spellings.

Note & accord taxonomy — the keystone

B-01
Specified · migration requiredFragranticaOakcha

Notes, accords, families, concentration, sillage and longevity move out of prose and into Shopify metaobjects and metafields, on one normalised vocabulary.

Inspired by

Fragrantica for the vocabulary — the industry's canonical note taxonomy; Oakcha for proving a facet set can be driven off it. BENCHMARK.md Part 1 §3.2

Why it converts

Indirectly, and completely. Filtering (A-01), the quiz (A-02), the note pyramid (B-02), the intensity scale (B-03), layering suggestions (C-02) and "similar scents" cannot be built on prose. This is the dependency the rest of the programme sits on.

Siwa's gap

Zero metafields and zero metaobjects exist today. 18 of 56 products carry parseable notes; the other 38 need authoring. Both Heart and Middle are in use for the same tier and must be normalised in migration.

Note pyramid

B-02
BuiltOakcha

Top, heart and base tiers rendered as three iconed rows, with the same three descriptors repeated compactly on the product card.

Inspired by

Oakcha for the three iconed tiers; Skylar for exactly three note descriptors on the card. DIRECTION.md Part 2 §3.3, §4.2C

Why it converts

It answers the single most-asked pre-purchase question in this category at the moment it is asked, instead of leaving the customer to message the brand and wait.

Siwa's gap

Instagram commenters repeatedly ask "notes?", "Details plz", "what is the notes for it". The brand's own Coco Woods post is already a finished note-pyramid graphic and the highest-engagement asset in the set — the design work exists and has never reached the website.

Intensity & sillage scales

B-03
BuiltData does not exist yetOakcha

Two labelled three-step scales — projection (Subtle / Distinct / Powerful) and longevity — each with a plain-language description rather than a bare bar.

Inspired by

Oakcha, which runs a Scent Intensity Scale and a separate Sillage Scale on every product page. DIRECTION.md Part 2 §3.1

Why it converts

Projection and longevity are the two properties buyers most often cite as the reason a fragrance disappointed. Stating them before purchase converts a post-purchase complaint into a pre-purchase filter — it protects margin through fewer returns as much as it lifts conversion.

Siwa's gap

Product descriptions mention longevity on 2 of 56 products. The scale is built; the underlying ratings are new metafield data that has to be authored.

Inspired-by price-contrast block

B-04
BuiltBlocked on counselOakcha

On the 40 inspired-by products only, one line directly beneath the price carrying three facts: the house, the fragrance, and the original's retail price. Never on the 16 originals.

Inspired by

Oakcha — "Inspired by MFK's Baccarat Rouge 540 (Retail price: $300)", placed above the notes. ALT. Fragrances runs the same play. BENCHMARK.md Part 1 §3.4 · DIRECTION.md Part 2 §3.2A

Why it converts

It makes the value gap the headline instead of an inference. A shopper who does not recognise the reference learns what they are getting; one who does recognise it is handed the price argument directly.

Siwa's gap

The reference is currently encoded in Shopify's vendor field — so Google reads Bleu De Chanel L'exclusif as the brand of the product. Siwa takes the legal exposure of naming Chanel and gets none of the merchandising benefit. Migration: vendor → Siwa Fragrances on all 56 SKUs, with inspired_by.house, .fragrance and .retail_price as metafields.

Blocker

Oakcha operates in the US; Siwa operates in Egypt. Get counsel before shipping the retail-price comparison. The module ships and works without that third fact — see §6.

Story-before-notes product page

B-05
BuiltD.S. & DurgaAmouage

A narrative block above the note data, plus a persona block tying the product to the quiz archetype it belongs to.

Inspired by

D.S. & Durga put story above notes in the page hierarchy; Amouage opens with storytelling before any product. BENCHMARK.md Part 2 §3B

Why it converts

Story is what justifies a price without discounting it, and it is the only defence a 56-SKU house has against being compared purely on millilitres.

Siwa's gap

Five of 56 products use the "Persona / The Story" format. It is the best copy in the catalogue and is used on 9% of it; 14 products carry under 200 characters of description in total.

Gallery, variant selector, price display

B-06
BuiltPhotography blocked

Zoom and lightbox gallery, a size selector that keeps sold-out variants visible for price anchoring, and locale-aware EGP formatting in both Latin and Arabic numerals.

Why it converts

Keeping a sold-out 100ml visible next to an in-stock 50ml anchors the smaller size as the reasonable choice rather than hiding the ladder. Correct bilingual price formatting removes a friction point Arabic-reading customers currently meet at the most sensitive moment on the page.

Siwa's gap

50 of 56 products have exactly one image, no variant has a featured image, and 100% of product images have null alt text — which disables the theme's own hover-second-image and variant-switching behaviour and costs the store image search entirely. This is a photography brief, not a code fix.

Track C

Basket building — the track with the most upside

Siwa's entry size is a 30ml at 375–850 EGP with no trial tier beneath it, three static bundle SKUs, and a free-shipping threshold that does nothing.

Bundle builder & discovery set

C-01
BuiltSnifALT.

Pick-three at travel size, priced as a set, with a tiered ladder above it — sample set → discovery trio → layering bundle → full bottle plus body.

Inspired by

Snif — three 10ml travel sizes for $68, layering bundles at $44–79, sample sets at $24/$30, scent bundles at $99. ALT. runs "3 for $99". DIRECTION.md Part 1 §2.5

Why it converts

Two mechanisms at once. It raises average order value on the customers who already convert, and it creates the missing top of funnel for the ones who do not — which for a house whose entire pitch is try this instead of the expensive bottle is the structurally obvious product to sell.

Siwa's gap

No sampling tier exists at any price. The three bundle SKUs on the store are fixed products, not a builder — and two of them carry overstated compare-at prices that should be corrected before any bundle is merchandised harder.

Layering suggestions

C-02
BuiltKayaliSnifSkylar

On any Layering-line product page: pick a base, see suggested partners, add both to the bag priced as the existing bundle SKU. Backed by a layering content hub on the blog.

Inspired by

Kayali's layering system — the line Siwa's own Layering products clone; Snif for fragrance-plus-mist bundle pricing; Skylar for the "Layering Lounge" content-hub pattern. BENCHMARK.md Part 1 §3.7 · DIRECTION.md Part 2 §4.2E

Why it converts

This is the lowest-effort revenue feature in the programme, because every component of it already exists as a product. The site sells the pieces and withholds the instructions; supplying the instructions turns a single-item purchase into a two-item one.

Siwa's gap

layering-vanilla is the #1 product in the catalogue by review volume — 98 reviews at 5.00★. A full Layering line exists (Apple, Lychee, Pistachio, Vanilla), a 30ml layering bundle SKU exists, and body splash and lotion exist as partners. There is no layering guidance anywhere on the site, and /blogs/news has zero articles.

Cart drawer with free-shipping progress

C-03
BuiltSnif

A slide-out cart with line-item editing, a progress bar to the 1,500 EGP free-shipping threshold showing exactly what remains, and a cross-sell rail beneath it.

Inspired by

Snif — free shipping over $75, used deliberately as a bundle driver rather than a footnote. DIRECTION.md Part 1 §2.5

Why it converts

A threshold only works if the shopper is told how far they are from it at the moment they can act. Shown as remaining balance next to a relevant add-on, it is the single most direct order-value lever on the site.

Siwa's gap

The 1,500 EGP threshold already exists and is currently communicated nowhere in the cart.

Gift messaging

C-04
BuiltSnifLe Labo

An optional gift note, recipient details and a hide-prices toggle at cart level.

Inspired by

Snif for gift messaging as a standard cart option; Le Labo for per-order personalised labelling as the premium version of the same idea. BENCHMARK.md Part 1 §1 Tier 3

Why it converts

Fragrance is a gift category. Without a gift path, every gift-intent visitor is either lost or converted into a lower-value self-purchase.

Wishlist

C-05
BuiltPersistence pendingLattafa

Save-for-later from the card and the product page, with a dedicated wishlist view.

Inspired by

Lattafa — the only reference in the regional tier that ships one, and the most transferable comparable for an Egyptian brand. BENCHMARK.md Part 1 §2

Why it converts

It captures the considered purchase instead of losing it, and gives a returning visitor a shortlist rather than a catalogue. It also pairs directly with restock alerts (D-04) for sold-out products.

Checkout CTA, quantity, discount code

C-06
Built

A primary checkout button with explicit loading and error states, trust signals placed next to it rather than in the footer, quantity controls with stock constraints, and coupon entry with clear applied and error states.

Why it converts

The last three metres of the funnel are where silent failures cost the most. A button with no loading state produces double-submits and abandonment; a coupon field with no error state produces a customer who assumes the discount applied and leaves when it did not.

Track D

Trust — deploying the asset the store already owns

1,212 reviews at 4.98★ across a 56-product catalogue is a higher review density than several of the benchmark set. It currently appears as a homepage carousel and nothing else.

aggregateRating structured data

D-01
Specified · ships independentlyOakchaALT.

Emit aggregateRating in each product's JSON-LD so Google can render star snippets in organic results.

Inspired by

Nobody in the Tier 1 comparable set ships without it. BENCHMARK.md Part 1 §3.1

Why it converts

It acts before the click, not after it. Stars in the search result raise click-through on listings the store already ranks for — meaning it lifts revenue without lifting rank, spend or traffic.

Siwa's gap

Verified zero occurrences across all 56 product pages. 54 of 56 products qualify today. This is the highest return-on-effort action available anywhere in the project, it is measured in hours, and it does not depend on the rebuild — it can ship on the current theme.

Reviews, loud

D-02
BuiltOakchaALT.

Rating summary with distribution histogram, a sortable review list with verified badges, review cards on product cards, a write-a-review flow, and a dedicated reviews landing page. Arabic review bodies render in the Arabic UI face.

Inspired by

Oakcha — 10,588 reviews surfaced on a single product with distribution and verified badges; ALT. leads its whole site with "50,000+ verified reviews". Explicitly not Amouage, which suppresses social proof. DIRECTION.md Part 1 §2.1

Not copied

Oakcha's AI-generated review summaries. Siwa's distribution is already unusual; a synthetic summary layered on a skewed corpus compounds a credibility problem rather than solving one.

Why it converts

Social proof is the cheapest substitute for the thing a fragrance page cannot offer — smelling it. Siwa's rating is high enough that surfacing it more aggressively is close to free.

Caveat we are carrying openly

804 of 820 attributable reviews are 5★ with none below 4★, under manual approval. This is documented as a credibility question to resolve before the corpus is promoted externally as a headline claim — see §6.

Trust badges — verifiable claims only

D-03
BuiltKahinaLattafa

Five badges placed at decision points rather than in the footer: verified review count, secure payment, the free-shipping threshold, Crafted in Egypt, and a single Cultural Accent أصيل · Authentic Siwan mark used on roughly two products.

Inspired by

Kahina — impact stated as countable facts and named partners rather than adjectives; Lattafa's "Spot a fake" authenticity guidance, directly relevant in a category full of counterfeits. DIRECTION.md Part 1 §2.3

Not copied

Oakcha's generic DTC badge row (vegan, paraben-free, atomizer quality). It is filler and it sits badly against a heritage register.

Why it converts

A specific claim carries weight a generic one does not. The authenticity badge is deliberately restricted — applied brand-wide it becomes decoration and stops meaning anything.

Siwa's gap

The current USP bar reads "Exquisite Ingredients / Inclusive Pricing / Personalized Service" — three adjectives with no evidence behind any of them.

Back-in-stock capture

D-04
Specified · not builtNo reference in the corpus

Email or WhatsApp capture on every sold-out variant, feeding a restock alert.

Inspired by

None — this is the one feature in the programme with no exemplar anywhere in the reference set. It is here on Siwa's own evidence. DIRECTION.md Part 2 §8

Why it converts

It is demand capture, not a convenience. Every sold-out product page is currently a dead end that converts a ready buyer into a lost one, and the demand is documented rather than assumed.

10 products are fully sold out and 49 of 158 variants are unavailable. The review corpus contains eight explicit restock pleas — citrine (20 reviews, 5.00★, tagged Best Selling, entirely sold out) has a customer writing "always sold out"; alluring-rose holds 24 reviews at 4.96★ with every variant out.

Track E

Identity — closing the gap between the bottle and the website

The physical product is a warm, bilingual, cinematic extrait house. The website is monochrome, all-caps, English-only and storyless. These are the features that reconcile them.

Bilingual EN/AR storefront with RTL

E-01
BuiltCopy authoring pendingLattafa

Every component ships in both scripts: mirrored grid and nav, Arabic display and UI faces at matching visual weight to their Latin counterparts, looser Arabic body leading, icons and chevrons that flip, and Arabic numeral formatting for prices.

Inspired by

Lattafa — full EN/AR bilingual with regional pricing, and commercial proof that bilingual works in this exact category. BENCHMARK.md Part 1 §3.9

Not copied

Widian reads as the bilingual reference and is not one — its Arabic is symbolic rather than functional. And bilingual is not multi-currency: Siwa is EGP single-market by design, and only the first is being built.

Why it converts

This is the largest audience expansion available without touching logistics, warehousing or pricing. It is also the reason it has to be designed in now rather than retrofitted — RTL added late means rebuilding every layout.

Siwa's gap

The Mawj bottle label reads SIWA FRAGRANCES → موج → EXTRAIT DE PARFUM. The Arabic is already on the physical product; the website is the only place it has been erased. This is identity restoration, not localisation.

House of Siwa — the story track

E-02
BuiltFueguiaAmouageKahina

Four narrative pillars — The Oasis · The Ingredients · The Craft · The House — built from image-with-text sections, a hero, editorial pull-quotes and a heritage block, promoted into the primary nav.

Inspired by

Fueguia for the four-pillar naming (Philosophy · Botanical Exploration · Creation · Galleries); Amouage for anchoring origin to real geography rather than adjectives; Kahina for making labour legible and dating the founding story. DIRECTION.md Part 1 §2.3, §2.4

Why it converts

Story is what lets a house hold price. The measurable part is narrower and more certain: two pages that currently receive no internal links at all will begin receiving traffic, and a brand that is currently indistinguishable from a generic dupe store gets a reason to be remembered and returned to.

Siwa's gap

The entire brand story is one generic paragraph on an orphan page. Meanwhile the real Siwa — Amazigh identity and the Siwi language, kershef salt-and-mud architecture, Shali Fortress, the Temple of the Oracle, the five-colour embroidery palette — is used nowhere on the site.

Provenance stamp and artisan credit

E-03
BuiltKahinaGoldfield & Banks

A stamped, passport-style provenance label for batch and origin facts, and a credited-maker line on the Originals line.

Inspired by

Kahina — the women's signatures and identity marks appear on the packaging, and the giving programme is stated in countable items with named partners. Goldfield & Banks credits Traditional Custodians explicitly. DIRECTION.md Part 1 §2.3

Why it converts

It converts decoration into attribution. A heritage claim that names a person, a place and a number is credible; the same claim as an adjective is not, and shoppers in this category discount adjectives automatically.

Boundary

This is the feature with a real ethical floor. A brand trading on the Siwan name should name the Siwans, and ideally source or commission from them. Cultural sign-off is a prerequisite, not a polish step — see §6.

Two-palette design system and the vintage layer

E-04
BuiltWidianSiwan embroidery

A calm sand-and-gold UI chrome palette that recedes behind photography, plus a five-colour Cultural Accent set drawn from real Siwan embroidery, firewalled out of nav, buttons, forms, checkout and price. On top of that a paper-grain, vignette and specimen-card treatment restricted to hero, editorial and badge surfaces.

Inspired by

Widian for translating architecture into object — Siwa's equivalent of their domes is kershef, the salt-clay-mud material Shali is built from. The five accent colours are inherited from Siwan embroidery: palm green, date red, date yellow, desert brown, black. DIRECTION.md Part 1 §2.2 · BENCHMARK.md Part 2 §2

Why it converts

The firewall is the commercial point. Heritage colour in the checkout makes a store look like a museum; heritage colour on the story page and nowhere else lets the brand feel specific while the buy path stays legible. It is also unrepeatable — a competitor can copy a gold accent, not a documented embroidery palette.

Accessibility

Three colour pairs in the specification fail WCAG AA and the library ships corrected values alongside the originals, shown side by side rather than silently swapped. Contrast failures on a price or a CTA are conversion failures before they are compliance ones.

USP bar rebuilt on verifiable claims

E-05
BuiltAmouageKahina

An icon-and-text row replacing the current three adjectives with facts a customer can check: shipping threshold, review count, origin, and returns.

Why it converts

It occupies the highest-traffic strip on the site — directly under the hero, seen by every visitor. Turning that strip from unverifiable adjectives into four checkable facts is one of the cheapest edits in the programme.

Track F

Retention — the second order

The only retention mechanic on the store today is a 50 EGP newsletter popup.

Referral

F-01
BuiltSnifSkylar

A share-and-earn widget with a personal link, native share sheet and WhatsApp as a first-class channel.

Inspired by

Skylar's "Give $20, Get $20" and Snif's retention stack. BENCHMARK.md Part 1 §3.10

Why it converts

It is the cheapest growth lever available to a brand with a 4.98★ base and demonstrated repeat demand — it monetises satisfaction that already exists rather than buying new traffic. WhatsApp matters specifically: it is how this market actually shares.

Announcement bar, newsletter, promo banner, countdown

F-02
Built

A dismissible bilingual announcement bar, email capture carrying the existing 50 EGP incentive, full-width promotional sections, and a countdown for genuinely time-boxed offers.

Why it converts

Email capture is the only owned channel in the stack — everything else is rented. The newsletter incentive already exists and simply needs placing at exit intent or scroll depth rather than on load, where it currently interrupts before any value has been shown.

Discipline

The countdown is for real deadlines only. A permanent countdown is a trust cost that outlives whatever urgency it borrowed.

Recommendations and cross-sell

F-03
Built

"You may also like" and "Complete the set" rails on the product page and in the cart, driven by the note taxonomy rather than by collection membership.

Why it converts

Recommendations built on shared accords are relevant; recommendations built on "same collection" are noise. This is another feature that only becomes real once B-01 exists.

Siwa's gap

Body splash and lotion products already exist and are barely merchandised — six SKUs on a separate template, in the category where Sol de Janeiro and Snif built their volume.

Subscription and loyalty

F-04
Specified · not builtSnifSkylar

A recurring programme with member pricing, early access and pause/cancel control.

Inspired by

Snif's subscription and Skylar's Scent Club — the two references in the set with real retention mechanics. BENCHMARK.md Part 1 §3.10

Why it converts

It changes the unit of sale from an order to a relationship, which is the only durable defence a dupe-tier house has against being undercut.

Sequencing

Deliberately last. Subscription on a catalogue with 10 sold-out products and no restock alerting would sell a promise the inventory cannot keep. D-04 and the stock position come first.
